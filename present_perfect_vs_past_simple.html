Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Grammar chart + tips to tell them apart

Past simple = a finished time (the "when" matters).
Present perfect = a connection to now (the "when" doesn't matter or isn't finished).

7 tips to tell them apart

1. Ask yourself: "Can I say WHEN?"

If there's a specific finished time (or you're asking for one) → past simple. If not → present perfect.

Past simpleI lost my keys yesterday.
Present perfectI've lost my keys. (I can't find them now!)
2. "Ago" = past simple. "Since" = present perfect.

Never use ago with the present perfect. Use since + a starting point with the present perfect.

Past simpleI moved here three years ago.
Present perfectI've lived here since 2023.
3. "For" works with both — but the meaning changes

Present perfect = it's still true. Past simple = it's over.

Past simpleI lived in Rio for 5 years. (I don't live there now.)
Present perfectI've lived in Rio for 5 years. (I still live there.)
4. Is the time period finished?

This morning, today, this week can go both ways. Is it still going on? → present perfect. Is it over? → past simple.

At 4 p.m.I drank three coffees this morning. (morning is over)
At 10 a.m.I've drunk three coffees this morning. (still morning)
5. Start with the present perfect, continue with the past simple

In conversation, we introduce an experience or news with the present perfect, then give the details (when, where, how) with the past simple.

— Have you ever been to Japan? — Yes, I have. I went there in 2019. It was amazing!

— I've finished the report! — Great! When did you finish it?

6. Life experience vs. a specific moment

"Ever / never" + present perfect is about your whole life up to now. A specific moment in that life → past simple.

Past simpleI tried sushi at my friend's party last year.
Present perfectI've never tried sushi.
7. If the person / situation is gone → past simple

When the person is dead or the situation has completely ended, there's no connection to now.

Past simpleShakespeare wrote many plays.
Present perfectStephen King has written more than 60 books.

Note: in informal American English, people sometimes use the past simple with just, already and yet ("Did you eat yet?"). The present perfect is always correct in these cases.
